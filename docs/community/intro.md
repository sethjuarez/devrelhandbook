A community responds.

DevRel grows community by creating moments where developers can learn, participate, give signal, and build trust with the people behind the technology.

## Moments create signal

Community work happens through moments.

A moment can be a flagship event, a small meetup, a livestream, a workshop, a user group, a GitHub discussion, a Discord thread, a one to one conversation, or a hallway question after a talk.

The size matters less than the signal.

Look for the pattern. Developers show up, ask better questions, trust the answer, try the technology, tell you where it hurt, and come back.

Those are community signals.

## Plan around people, problems, and principles

Community planning starts with three questions.

| Lens | Question |
| --- | --- |
| People | Who are the developers we need to serve? |
| Problems | What are they trying to do, and where do they get stuck? |
| Principles | What do we believe about how to help them? |

This keeps community work from becoming a calendar of disconnected activity.

An event without a clear audience is noise. A community channel without a problem to organize around becomes support overflow. A program without principles becomes whatever the loudest request wants it to be.

## Grow trust in rings

Some stories need private technical testing before public launch.

Some ideas need to move through rings of trust. Start with internal builders. Then bring in a small group of trusted technical users. Then partners or customers. Then the public story.

Each ring makes the story stronger.

The team learns before the blast radius gets large. A trusted technical audience can tell you when the story is confusing, when the sample is hard to reproduce, when the product truth is not ready, and when the value is real.

## Community feeds the loop

Community connects back to content and technology.

If the same question appears in every meetup, write the guide. If the same setup fails in every workshop, fix the sample or file the product issue. If the community keeps using the product in a way the team did not expect, learn from it.

Community is where the organization hears the developer's side of the story.

It has to be more than sentiment. It has to feed the loop.

## The thin parts need care

Community work becomes vague when the team leaves the practice undefined.

Cadence, moderation, feedback triage, contributor programs, office hours, and community health all need their own operating detail. This handbook starts with the strategic role of community because that is the part most teams skip.

The daily work still matters.

Grow community in a way that creates trust, helps developers make progress, and gives the organization signal it can act on.
